# Register levers

Contents: [Raise complexity](#raise-complexity) | [Lower complexity](#lower-complexity) | [Language mechanisms](#language-mechanisms) | [Sources](#sources)

Every lever obeys the invariants in `SKILL.md`. A lever that would change a claim, a certainty marker, or an actor does not apply to that sentence.

## Raise complexity

Apply strongest first. Academic density comes from compressed noun phrases, not from piling up subordinate clauses (Biber and Gray 2010).

1. **Nominalize processes.** Turn verbs and adjectives into abstract nouns and link them with a relational verb.
   - "Prices rose, so people bought less." becomes "The increase in prices induced a contraction in consumption."
   - "Cuando el agua se evapora rápido, las sales precipitan." becomes "La rápida evaporación del agua produce la precipitación de las sales."
2. **Compress into noun phrases.** Fold relative and adverbial clauses into premodifiers, prepositional chains, and appositives attached to one head noun.
   - "the method that researchers developed after years of tests" becomes "the empirically derived, multi-year experimental method"
3. **Shift to the learned lexical stratum.** Prefer Latinate or Greek forms, or the language's equivalent prestige layer (see the table below).
   - "use" becomes "utilize", "end" becomes "terminate", and "look at" becomes "examine".
4. **Make abstractions the subject.** Promote concepts, data, or methods to subject position: "The findings indicate", "The analysis reveals". Use passive or impersonal forms only when the source names no actor.
5. **Use light-verb constructions.** "analyze" becomes "carry out an analysis of", "anwenden" becomes "zur Anwendung bringen", and "研究" becomes "进行研究".
6. **Use formal connectors.** "so" becomes "consequently", "but" becomes "nevertheless", "also" becomes "furthermore", and "pero" becomes "no obstante".
7. **Merge sentences.** Join related sentences with participial or absolute constructions: "Given the variability of the results, the analysis requires..."
8. **Substitute established terminology.** Replace a descriptive phrase with its standard technical term. Never coin a term.

Not levers: adding hedges ("may", "it could be argued"), adding citations, and hyperbaton that breaks parsing. The first two change meaning. The third breaks grammar.

## Lower complexity

Apply strongest first.

1. **Unpack nominalizations.** Turn abstract nouns back into verbs. "The implementation of the policy" becomes "When they put the policy in place".
2. **Restore actors and active voice.** Make the actor the subject when the source names it or the context makes it certain. Otherwise use an impersonal active form. Never invent an actor.
3. **One idea per sentence.** Split stacked noun phrases and long sentences into short ones in logical or time order.
4. **Use common words.** "utilize" becomes "use", and "terminate" becomes "end". Keep a technical term the reader needs and explain it once in plain words.
5. **Use everyday connectors.** "consequently" becomes "so", and "nevertheless" becomes "but".
6. **Expand appositives.** Turn a compressed definition into its own sentence.
7. **Cut empty metadiscourse.** Delete "It is important to note that" and similar frames that carry no claim. Keep every hedge.
8. **Level 1 only (easy-to-read).** Apply these extra rules from easy-to-read standards:
   - Use the same word for the same thing throughout.
   - Replace metaphors and idioms with literal wording.
   - Write numbers as digits.
   - Put steps and options in lists.
   - Address the reader directly only when the source addresses the reader.

## Language mechanisms

Each language raises register through its own grammar. Use this table to pick the form of each lever. To lower complexity, use the reverse of each entry.

| Language | Nominalization | Learned stratum | Impersonal or formal markers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| English | -tion, -ment, -ity, -ance nouns | Latinate and Greek over Germanic | Agentless passive, "it is" frames |
| Spanish | -ción, -miento, -idad nouns with "de" chains | Latin cultismos | Pasiva refleja ("se analizaron"), "el cual" relatives |
| French | -tion, -ment, -ité nouns with "de" chains | Latinate learned forms | Participial and absolute clauses, "on" |
| German | Nominalstil, -ung, -heit, -keit, compounds | Fremdwörter over native words | Funktionsverbgefüge, extended participial attributes before the noun |
| Chinese | Verb in noun slot, clause + 的 + noun | Disyllabic over monosyllabic words, chéngyǔ | Light verbs 进行 and 作出, classical 之, 鉴于 |
| Japanese | Kango noun compounds without する | Kango over wago | である style over です/ます, taigendome |
| Korean | -음/ㅁ nominalizer | Hanja-eo over native words | 해라체 plain style |
| Arabic | Masdar, idafa chains | Classical lexis within MSA | Periphrastic passive with تم |

- Keep the source variety. Moving colloquial Arabic to MSA, or casual Japanese to a different politeness level, changes the variety. Do it only when the user asks.
- In languages without derivational suffixes, such as Chinese, nominalize by position and by light verbs.
